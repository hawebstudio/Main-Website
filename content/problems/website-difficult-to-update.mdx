# Website Is Difficult to Update

A website should make publishing content easier — not harder. If every small change requires developer support or editing content feels complicated, the website becomes difficult to maintain and often falls behind the business.

Modern content management systems allow teams to update pages, publish articles, manage products, and organize media without modifying application code.

See the connected [service-led solutions](/services) for the implementation approach behind this challenge.

## Signs Your Website Is Difficult to Maintain

Your website may need a better content management system if:

- Every content change requires a developer.
- Publishing a new page takes hours instead of minutes.
- Staff avoid updating the website.
- Content becomes outdated.
- Editing frequently breaks layouts.
- The CMS feels slow or confusing.

These issues reduce efficiency and make it harder to keep the website relevant.

## Common Causes

Content management problems often result from:

- Legacy website architecture
- Hard-coded pages
- Poor content modeling
- Outdated CMS platforms
- Plugin conflicts
- No editorial workflow
- Poor user permissions
- Limited flexibility

Many of these issues become more noticeable as the business grows.

## What Should Be Improved?

A modern CMS should provide:

- Easy page editing
- Structured content
- Media management
- Draft and publish workflows
- Role-based permissions
- Flexible content models
- Reliable performance
- Long-term scalability

The goal is to reduce dependency on developers for routine updates.

## Our Approach

We first understand how your team works with content.

This includes reviewing:

- Publishing workflow
- Content types
- Editorial process
- Existing CMS
- Business requirements
- Future growth plans
- User permissions
- Integration requirements

Based on these findings, we recommend the CMS that best fits your business rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all solution.

## Decision Framework

Not every business needs the same CMS.

- **WordPress** is often well suited for traditional business websites and blogs.
- **Sanity** works well for modern, headless websites with structured content.
- **Payload CMS** is ideal for custom platforms requiring greater backend control.

The right choice depends on your content strategy, technical requirements, and long-term goals.
