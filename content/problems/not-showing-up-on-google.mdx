# Not Showing Up on Google

Launching a website does not automatically make it visible in search results. Search engines must first discover your website, crawl its pages, understand their content, and determine that they are relevant and authoritative enough to show to searchers. If any step in this chain breaks down, your website becomes invisible — even if it looks great and has excellent content.

This is one of the most common and frustrating problems business owners face. You have invested time and money into building a website, but when you search for your services on Google, you are nowhere to be found. Meanwhile, competitors with arguably worse websites appear on the first page.

## Why This Problem Costs You More Than You Think

Search engines are the primary way people find businesses, services, and information online. When your website does not appear for relevant searches, you are not just missing out on traffic — you are missing out on **qualified, high-intent visitors** who are actively looking for what you offer.

Consider the compounding cost:

- A local service business might receive 50–200 relevant searches per month for their core services
- If your website appeared for those searches with a 5% click-through rate, that is 2.5–10 potential leads per month
- Over a year, that is 30–120 qualified enquiries you never received
- If even 10% of those convert, you are losing 3–12 customers annually to competitors who rank above you

Unlike paid advertising, organic search traffic compounds over time. The longer you wait to fix visibility issues, the further behind you fall.

## Common Root Causes — Explained

Search visibility problems rarely have a single cause. Here are the most common technical and content issues we encounter:

**No sitemap or poor crawl configuration**: Without an XML sitemap and proper robots.txt file, search engines cannot efficiently discover all your important pages. Some pages may never be found at all.

**Metadata gaps**: Missing or duplicate title tags and meta descriptions mean Google cannot understand what each page is about or generate compelling search result snippets.

**Poor site architecture**: If your website has a flat or disorganised structure, search engines struggle to understand the relationship between pages and which ones are most important.

**Thin or duplicate content**: Pages with very little content, or content that duplicates what is on other pages, provide no value to search engines. They may be ignored entirely.

**Technical barriers**: Issues like broken internal links, slow page speed, JavaScript rendering problems, canonical tag errors, and HTTP/HTTPS mixed content can all prevent proper indexing.

**No structured data**: Without schema markup, search engines have to guess what your content means. Structured data helps them understand your business, services, locations, and FAQs — and can earn you rich results in search.

## How to Diagnose It Yourself

Before engaging an SEO professional, you can run a basic diagnostic:

1. **Check indexation**: Search `site:yourdomain.com` on Google. The number of results tells you how many pages Google has indexed. Compare this to the total number of pages on your site.

2. **Review Search Console**: If you have Google Search Console set up, check the Pages report for errors, warnings, and excluded pages. Look at Performance data for impressions and clicks.

3. **Test page speed**: Run your homepage through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev). Scores below 70 indicate performance problems that affect both user experience and rankings.

4. **Check mobile experience**: Browse your website on your phone. If it is difficult to navigate, text is too small, or buttons are hard to tap, you have mobile usability issues.

5. **Search for your services**: Google the exact services you offer in your area. If you do not appear in the first two pages of results, you have a visibility gap.
