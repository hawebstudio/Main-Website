# Customers Can't Find You Locally

When someone needs a service in their area — whether it's a dentist, a marketing consultant, or a roofing contractor — they pull out their phone and search Google. They almost always choose one of the businesses that appear at the very top, specifically in the "Local Pack" (the map section showing three local businesses).

If your business isn't showing up there, those customers aren't just delaying their decision; they are actively choosing your competitors right now. Local search is highly competitive, but it is also the highest-intent traffic you can capture.

## The Cost of Local Invisibility

Local searches have incredible conversion rates. Consider someone searching for "emergency plumber near me" or "corporate caterer in [City]". These people have an immediate need and money to spend. 

If your business relies on local customers, being invisible on Google Maps and local search results means:
- You are missing out on 'ready-to-buy' customers every single day.
- You have to spend more on paid advertising to capture the same leads.
- Newer or lower-quality competitors who understand local SEO will dominate your market share.

## Why Your Business Isn't Appearing Locally

Local SEO functions differently than traditional website SEO. Google uses a distinct algorithm to determine map rankings, relying heavily on proximity, relevance, and prominence. Common reasons you aren't ranking include:

**Unclaimed or Unoptimised Google Business Profile (GBP)**: Your GBP is the single most important factor for local search. If it isn't claimed, fully filled out with accurate categories, and actively managed, Google won't trust it enough to display it prominently.

**Inconsistent NAP Data**: Name, Address, and Phone number (NAP). If your business is listed as "Smith & Sons" on Facebook, "Smith and Sons LLC" on Yelp, and has a different phone number on your website, Google gets confused and lowers your ranking.

**Lack of Local Website Content**: Your website and your GBP are connected. If your website doesn't clearly state where you are and what areas you serve, it fails to support your map listing.

**Poor Review Velocity and Sentiment**: It's not just about having 5-star reviews. It's about how often you get them (velocity) and whether you respond to them. A competitor getting one review a week will often outrank a business that got fifty reviews three years ago and none since.

**Missing Local Structured Data**: Search engines rely on code called "schema markup" to definitively understand your business type, address, and service areas. If your site lacks LocalBusiness schema, you are making Google work harder to understand you.
