# Technologies

## Technology Stack Philosophy

HA Web Studio chooses technologies based on business requirements, maintainability, performance, accessibility, integration quality, and long-term ownership cost. A tool is useful only when it helps the website or application do its job after launch.

## How Technologies Are Selected

Technology decisions are made during discovery and architecture planning. The selection process considers content workflow, hosting model, security, search visibility, developer experience, operational support, and how often the system is expected to change.

## Business Requirements Before Trends

The goal is not to use the newest tool. The goal is to build a system that is understandable, reliable, fast, accessible, and realistic to maintain. This is why the same content system connects technologies to services, work, case studies, and insights.

## Why This Page Exists

Most visitors to this section aren't comparing frameworks for fun — they're trying to answer a
practical question before hiring a developer or agency: does this team actually understand the
tools involved, and can they explain the trade-offs in plain terms rather than jargon. Each
category page below breaks down what a group of technologies is for, when it's genuinely the
right choice, and when a simpler option would serve the project better. Nothing here is chosen
to sound impressive — every technology listed is one this studio actually uses in real project
work, tied to the services and case studies where it was actually applied.

Categories are organized around what a technology does in a project, not around marketing
labels, so a business owner without a technical background can still work out roughly where a
tool fits — frontend for what a visitor sees, backend for what happens behind the scenes,
infrastructure for where things run, and so on. This mirrors how project decisions actually get
made: a technology choice is proposed, the trade-offs are explained in plain terms, and the
client makes an informed call rather than being handed a list of unfamiliar product names with
no context for why any of them were selected.
